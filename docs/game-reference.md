## 1. How the game works

- **Dice.** Every roll is a number of dice, and each die counts only if it shows a **six** (one chance in six). Ten dice give about 1.7 hits.
- **Fighting.** Attack dice = Strength + the attack dice of everything worn. Defence dice = Dexterity + the defence dice of everything worn. Both lose one die when you are drunk, high or chafing. Damage = attack hits - defence hits (the defence is capped by *Base Maximum Defend*), then capped by the **damage limit**: the worn items' limits added together if any worn item has one (note: chainmail has one), otherwise the creature's *Unarmed Maximum Damage*. A hit is wounds: HP left = HP - Wounds. At 0 HP you die.
- **Wear.** Dealing N damage wears your weapons N times, and an enemy attack of N wears your armour N times; each point of wear falls on a random worn piece that can wear. Broken pieces vanish ("Yer X breaks!").
- **Enemies hit back** after most things you do in a fight (fight, run that fails, items and spells that act on an enemy, intimidation): each enemy in the place gets a counter-attack, physical or mental by its weights. Mental attacks add stress; at 0 MP you panic: you drop everything, lose half your money and wake in the town square.
- **Intimidation.** Your Influence dice against the enemy's Willpower dice; a win costs it 1 MP, at 0 MP it runs away (no loot, no XP). Only possible against a single enemy that has Willpower (skeletons and zombies have none), and only if you have Influence.
- **Experience.** Kills give XP. At the goal you level: the goal doubles, you get 1 point to assign (Strength, Dexterity, Influence, Willpower, Power, HP, MP or Mana), and wounds, stress and fatigue clear.
- **Moving.** Each step adds 1 hunger (more if you are high or food-poisoned) and lowers drunkenness, highness, food poisoning and chafing by 1. At 100 hunger it drops to 50 and you lose 1 HP. Carrying more than **50 + 10 x Strength** encumbrance stops you moving at all. Locked doors need the matching key, which is used up.
- **Food rots.** Every step you take, each item lying on the floor you leave gets its decay roll: fresh food has a 1 in 3 chance to turn rotten; rotten food has a 1 in 2 chance to vanish, and half of those leave a rat.
- **Randomness.** One seeded generator drives everything. The same seed (shown on the Status page, enterable under *Start with seed...*) always gives the same world; play differs only if your actions differ.

### In the game: what you can learn about creatures
- The **Enemies** screen lists each enemy here as `Name(current HP/max HP)`.
- Fight messages show every roll: your attack, its defence, damage, "has N HP left", and on its turn its attack, your defence and the damage. From those you can work out its dice.
- Intimidation messages show influence and willpower rolls and "loses 1 MP"; a creature with no willpower cannot be frightened at all (the *Intimidate!* button never appears).
- Kill messages show the money and XP it was worth; the loot lies on the floor afterwards (check **Ground...**).
- The dungeon view draws a **picture** of the first enemy in the place (every creature except the player has its own sprite, drawn in its own colour), and the **Map** marks places with enemies in pink.
- Creature names, bribes and parting shots are only learned by trying: using beer, a scroll or a bottle on an enemy that does not want it says nothing useful, and the items' *Use* is only offered when it can work (the item menu says "You cannot use that now!" otherwise).

### In the game: what you can learn about items
- **Inventory** groups identical names with a count and shows *Encumbrance: carried/maximum*; the item menu shows that item's *Encumbrance*.
- **Equipment** lists worn items; the name turns **yellow** when the wear is half the durability and **red** at a quarter left; the slot's detail page shows *Durability: current/max*.
- Shops show **buy prices** (Prices, Buy), **sell offers** (Offers, Sell) and **repair costs** (Repair). Only what you can afford is listed under Buy.
- Floor items are drawn as small coloured letters in the dungeon view (each item type has its own letter and colour, for example a key is a `K`, the colour telling which key); **Ground...** lists them by name, and the **Map** colours places with items purple.
- *Use* explains itself through its messages. Dice-based effects are not shown beforehand. Attack and defence dice, damage limits and the stats of items are **not** shown anywhere in the game; they are discovered by fighting or by this document.

## 4. Places and routes

- **Town:** the Town Square in the middle and eight Town places around it (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW), all connected by plain roads. One more place, the **Church Entrance**, is attached to a random town place on a free compass side. Each town place may hold one townsperson. Under the Innkeeper's place there is the **Cellar** (down).
- **Dungeon:** five levels (Level I to V), each an 11 x 11 maze with exactly one route between any two rooms (no loops). A room with one exit is a **dead end**; one random dead end per level is the **boss room**. The church entrance leads down into a random non-dead-end room of Level I; each level's boss room leads down into a random room of the next level. Every dead end is behind a **locked door** (FE door) needing an **FE Key**, found lying around the level; the boss room's door needs that level's own key: Level I **CU Key** (a dead end), II **AG Key** (a dead end), III **AU Key** (a dead end), IV **PT Key** (a dead end), V the **Elemental Orb**, which nothing in the game creates, so the Level V boss room is currently unreachable (see the data notes in section 3).
- **The Moon:** an 11 x 11 grid that wraps around at the edges; reached only by a **Moon Portal** (costly: 5,000 at the Magic shop).

**Route types** (what the door in the dungeon view shows): plain road/corridor/stairs show nothing; the letters `FE`, `CU`, `AG`, `AU`, `PT` and `EO` on a door name the key it needs (green `CU`, purple `AG`, yellow `AU`, cyan `PT`; `FE` is black). A route lost to a portal is a purple swirl picture. Portals are single use: walking through one removes it.

### In the game: what you can learn about places
- The header of the in-play screen names the place in town and shows *Facing* and *Exits* (compass letters). In the dungeon you see the corridor ahead drawn in perspective, side passages as diagonals, stairs up and down as marks on the ceiling and floor, and the facing letter at the top.
- **Map** (dungeon places only) draws every place you have visited at its true position, with its exits. Pink = enemies there, green = stairs, purple = items, black = nothing. The place you stand in is inverted. Unvisited places are never shown.
- Level, column and row of a place are not shown; use the map to find your way.

## 6. Spells and quests

### Spells

- **Holy Bolt** (max level 1); Power needed per level: level 0: 0, level 1: 1.
- **Purify** (max level 1); Power needed per level: level 0: 0, level 1: 0.
  - *Holy Bolt*: needs mana and an **undead** enemy in the place. Costs 1 fatigue; damage = your Power + spell level dice, each counting on a six. Taught by the *Book of Holy Bolt* (100 at the Black Mage).
  - *Purify*: needs mana. Costs 1 fatigue; turns every rotten food in your pack and worn items back into fresh food. Taught by the *Book of Purify* (50 at the Black Mage).
- Mana left = Mana - Fatigue; fatigue is cleared by levelling up and Mana is restored by the Black Mage (Restore) or the Herb. Spells that cannot be cast show **red** in the Spell List.

### Quests
- **Cellar Rats** (Graham the Innkeeper): accepting puts 1 rat in the cellar the first time, 2 the second time, and so on. Rat Tails (each rat drops one) are paid 1 money each, up to 10 per hand-in. Completion needs no tails but pays only for those you carry.
- **The main quest** (told by the Elder): kill **Kordanor** in the Level V boss room and bring the **Horns of Kordanor** (his only loot) to the Elder. The game has no ending: handing in the horns is not implemented, and the boss room cannot currently be reached (the Elemental Orb that opens its door is never created).
